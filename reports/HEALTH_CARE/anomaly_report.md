# Part 1 - For business readers

## The short version

- **Claim payments have dropped sharply, and it points to a gap in incoming claims.** The weekly payment runs have been winding down since early August. Almost all of the drop is payments on brand-new claims. No claims for visits after 30 June have reached our data, so the payment runs are working through an older backlog that is nearly used up.
- **June brought an unusual burst of mid-year enrollments.** 95 new eligibility records started in June, against a usual 16 to 30 for a month outside January. The cause is not yet known.
- **Much of our data stops at the end of June.** Claims, visits, pharmacy, billing and enrollment all stop between June and early July. Nothing new of any kind has been loaded since early September. Most measures looked normal up to that point, but we can't see anything after it.

**Claim Payments Disbursed (number of payments)**: 58 payments in the latest weekly run (1 September)

## 10. Caveats

- Every current reading describes June 2026 or earlier (payments: 1 September). The "normal" verdicts say nothing about July to September.
- Yearly marts have 5 reference years, so their baselines and bounds are informative only. Their 2026 rows are partial and excluded.
- Monthly series (78 points) were too short for the trained forecaster to beat naive.
- The trend_band forecasts don't re-apply seasonality.
- No business-dimension attribution was possible for payment $ or pharmacy fills (no usable dimensions). Normal KPIs were deliberately not attributed.
- Directionality is inferred for all 29 KPIs and needs human confirmation.
- `ACCOUNT_USAGE` has latency of up to about 3 hours and 365-day retention. It was not needed this run.
