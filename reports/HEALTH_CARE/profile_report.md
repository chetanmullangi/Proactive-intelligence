## 1. Inventory summary

`HEALTH_CARE` has 2 non-system schemas: `MAIN` (37 tables, all real content) and `PUBLIC` (empty). It's a standard payer-style claims warehouse:

- **Dimensions**: `DIM_MEMBER` (3,000), `DIM_PROVIDER` (3,079), `DIM_PROVIDER_LOCATION` (5,148), `DIM_PLAN` (23), `DIM_PRODUCT` (8), `DIM_NETWORK`, `DIM_MEMBER_GROUP`, `DIM_BENEFIT_STRUCTURE`, `BRIDGE_PROVIDER_NETWORK` (11,282)
- **Core transactional facts**: `MEDICAL_CLAIM_HEADER` (99,271 rows), `MEDICAL_CLAIM_LINE` (281,394), `FACT_CLAIM_DIAGNOSIS` (221,428), `FACT_CLAIM_PAYMENT` (76,147), `FACT_ENCOUNTER` (78,566), `PHARMACY_CLAIM` (137,410), `FACT_PREMIUM` (139,579), `FACT_MEMBER_MONTH` (137,652), `MEMBER_ELIGIBILITY` (14,271)
- **Marts (pre-aggregated, annual/monthly grain)**: `MART_MEMBER_YEAR_SUMMARY`, `MART_PROVIDER_YEAR_SUMMARY`, `MART_PHARMACY_CLASS_YEAR`, `MART_PMPM_PLAN_MONTH`, `MART_UTILIZATION_PER_1000`
- **Reference/lookup**: `REF_CLAIM_STATUS`, `REF_DATE`, `REF_DENIAL_REASON`, `REF_DIAGNOSIS`, `REF_DRUG`, `REF_PLACE_OF_SERVICE`, `REF_PROCEDURE`, `REF_SOURCE_SYSTEM`
- **Operational/audit**: `AUDIT_LOAD_LOG`, `DQ_RULE_RESULT`, `STAGE_FILE_LIST` (load pipeline bookkeeping)

Most of the data volume lives in the claim-line and diagnosis tables (`MEDICAL_CLAIM_LINE` 281k rows, `FACT_CLAIM_DIAGNOSIS` 221k rows). **Notable date-range finding**: the underlying claim/encounter data runs from `2020-01-01` through roughly `2026-06-30`–`2026-09-01` depending on table, while the account's wall-clock "today" is `2026-09-15` — see Caveats.

`MEMBER_ELIGIBILITY` (14,271 rows) is a **slowly-changing eligibility segment table**: one row per member per eligibility "version" (`VERSION_NUMBER`, `EFFECTIVE_DATE`/`TERMINATION_DATE`, `IS_CURRENT_FLAG`), covering all 3,000 members in `DIM_MEMBER`, spanning `EFFECTIVE_DATE` 2020-01-01 to 2026-06-01 (78 distinct month-start dates). `CHANGE_REASON` shows most rows (8,257 of 14,271, 58%) are `ANNUAL_RENEWAL`, with `NEW_ENROLLMENT` (3,000 — one per member), `PLAN_CHANGE` (1,671), `RE_ENROLLMENT` (623), and smaller categories (`GROUP_CHANGE`, `MEDICARE_AGE_IN`, `PRODUCT_CHANGE_TO_INDIVIDUAL`, `DUPLICATE_834_LOAD`, `ELIGIBILITY_CORRECTION`) rounding it out.

`FACT_CLAIM_DIAGNOSIS` (221,428 rows) is a **claim-line-adjacent fact table**: one row per diagnosis code recorded on a claim (`CLAIM_DIAGNOSIS_ID`, `CLAIM_ID`, `MEMBER_ID`, `DIAGNOSIS_SEQUENCE` 1–9, `DIAGNOSIS_KEY`/`DIAGNOSIS_CODE`, `DIAGNOSIS_TYPE` PRINCIPAL/SECONDARY, `IS_POA_FLAG`, `SERVICE_START_DATE`, `CALENDAR_YEAR`). `DIAGNOSIS_TYPE` splits 55%/45% SECONDARY/PRINCIPAL (122,455 / 98,973), and `DIAGNOSIS_SEQUENCE` decays from 98,973 rows at sequence 1 down to 42 at sequence 9. `IS_POA_FLAG` is null on 83% of rows (184,091 of 221,428) — present-on-admission is only meaningful for inpatient encounters, so it isn't usable as a database-wide rate.

`DIM_MEMBER` (3,000 rows, one row per member) and `DIM_PROVIDER_LOCATION` (5,148 rows, one row per provider practice location) are both now covered as KPI sources as of pass 8 (§3e) — see that section for their grain and measures.

### 3a. `FACT_PREMIUM` — lookback-window fix (pass 4)

`FACT_PREMIUM` (139,579 rows) represents **premium billing**, not payment received: `TRANSACTION_TYPE` is `PREMIUM` (137,652 rows) or `RETRO_ADJUSTMENT` (1,927 rows), `TRANSACTION_STATUS` is uniformly `POSTED`, and the measure column is `BILLED_PREMIUM_AMOUNT` — one row per member per coverage month. Its native grain is genuinely monthly (`COVERAGE_MONTH`, a `DATE` truncated to the first of the month), spanning `2020-01-01` to `2026-06-01` (78 distinct months, `span_days = 2343`).

**The bug**: a prior run's `series_sql` used `DATEADD(day, -90, MAX(COVERAGE_MONTH))`, which on a monthly-grain table only spans ~3 calendar months — well under the ~12-bucket viability bar — so the table was auto-excluded (see `skipped` note in earlier manifest versions). This wasn't a data problem; it was a fixed day-based window applied to a table whose native grain isn't daily/weekly.

**The fix**: `series_sql` now uses `DATEADD(month, -30, MAX(COVERAGE_MONTH))`, yielding **31 monthly buckets** (2023-12 through 2026-06) — well above the bar, and consistent with how `monthly_trend_sql`/`yearly_trend_sql` already scanned full history for every other KPI in this manifest.

Two KPIs added:
- **`main.fact_premium.sum_billed_premium_amount`** — total premium billed per month. Rising steadily from ~$1.48M (2023-12) to ~$2.27M (2026-06); full-history monthly regression r²=0.97 (slope +$20,889/month), the strongest monthly fit of any KPI in this database. Recent-4-vs-prior-4-month change: +6.2%.
- **`main.fact_premium.count_star`** — count of billed member-months per month. Rising from 1,913 to 2,162 over the same window; monthly r²=0.96. Recent-4-vs-prior-4-month change: +1.0%.

Both have a `COVERAGE_TYPE` dimension breakdown (4 values: `MEDICARE`, `COMMERCIAL`, `MEDICAID`, `EXCHANGE` — already human-readable, no join needed). The breakdown shows growth is **broad-based across all four segments**, not concentrated in one: `MEDICARE` billed amount grew the most in dollar terms (+$1.78M) and `COMMERCIAL` grew the most in member-month count (+845), but no segment is declining or dominating disproportionately.

**Caveat surfaced by this fix**: `month_of_year_seasonality_sql` shows billed amount split cleanly into two bands — Jan–Jun months average ~$10.4M (summed across all years) vs. Jul–Dec months averaging ~$8.3M. This is **not a real seasonal cycle** — it's an artifact of 2026 only having Jan–Jun data so far (7 occurrences of each Jan–Jun calendar month across 2020–2026, vs. only 6 occurrences of each Jul–Dec month, since no Jul–Dec 2026 data exists yet). Flagged in `month_of_year_seasonal: true` with this caveat in `score_rationale` so `snowflake-kpi-anomaly-detector` doesn't mistake it for genuine seasonality. `count_star`'s yearly-grain fit is correspondingly weak (r²=0.005, 7 buckets) for the same reason — the partial 2026 bucket drags down an otherwise-clean trend.

### 3b. `FACT_MEMBER_MONTH` — lookback-window fix (pass 5)

`FACT_MEMBER_MONTH` (137,652 rows) represents **member-months of enrollment/eligibility**: one row per member per calendar month they had active coverage. Confirmed by querying the table directly rather than assuming from naming — `MEMBER_MONTH_COUNT` is a flag-like column always equal to `1` (min=max=avg=1.000000), so `COUNT(*)` and `SUM(MEMBER_MONTH_COUNT)` are equivalent and both represent total enrolled member-months. Other columns: `PLAN_ID`/`PRODUCT_ID`/`GROUP_ID`/`NETWORK_ID`/`BENEFIT_STRUCTURE_ID` (foreign keys, no premium/cost amount lives on this table — that's `FACT_PREMIUM`'s job), `COVERAGE_TYPE`/`COVERAGE_TIER` (categorical), `AGE_BAND`/`GENDER_CODE`/`UTILIZATION_COHORT` (member demographic/risk segmentation), and `RISK_SCORE` (a continuous acuity score, avg ≈1.339 across the table). Native grain is monthly (`MONTH_START_DATE`), spanning `2020-01-01` to `2026-06-01` (78 distinct months, `span_days = 2343`) — an identical horizon to `FACT_PREMIUM`, consistent with both being generated from the same enrollment backbone.

**The bug (identical to `FACT_PREMIUM`, pass 4)**: a fixed 90-day lookback anchored on `MAX(MONTH_START_DATE)` yields only ~3 monthly buckets on this genuinely monthly-grain table, well under the ~12-bucket viability bar — a lookback-sizing bug, not a data problem. This was the exact gap flagged as the top priority in pass 4's `skipped` entry for this table.

**The fix**: `series_sql` now uses `DATEADD(month, -30, MAX(MONTH_START_DATE))`, yielding **31 monthly buckets** (2023-12 through 2026-06) — the same window size used for `FACT_PREMIUM`, for consistency.

One KPI added:
- **`main.fact_member_month.count_star`** ("Enrolled Member-Months") — count of active member-months per month. Rising from 1,888 (2023-12) to 2,137 (2026-06); full-history monthly regression r²=0.96 (slope +10.73 member-months/month). Recent-4-vs-prior-4-month change: +0.94%. This is the enrollment denominator that PMPM/utilization-per-1000 style metrics (see the still-`skipped` `MART_PMPM_PLAN_MONTH`/`MART_UTILIZATION_PER_1000`) would divide into — a foundational metric even though its own trend is unremarkable (steady growth, no anomaly).

Has a `COVERAGE_TYPE` dimension breakdown (same 4 values as `FACT_PREMIUM`, already human-readable). Growth is **broad-based across all four segments** — `COMMERCIAL` grew the most in absolute member-months (+839), followed by `MEDICARE` (+639), `MEDICAID` (+406), and `EXCHANGE` (+290) — the same pattern and proportions seen on the companion `FACT_PREMIUM` KPIs, which is itself a useful cross-table consistency check (enrollment growth and billed-premium growth are moving together, as they should).

**A second candidate measure was considered and rejected**: `AVG(RISK_SCORE)` was evaluated as a population-acuity KPI on this table but its monthly values are nearly constant (1.331–1.357 across all 78 months of history, coefficient of variation ≈0.01) with only weak signal at month grain (r²=0.21). Per the "drop near-constant series" rule, this doesn't clear the bar for an informative KPI right now — recorded in `skipped` rather than silently discarded, since a future material shift in risk mix would be exactly the kind of thing this metric should eventually catch.

**Same Jan–Jun/Jul–Dec artifact as `FACT_PREMIUM`**: `month_of_year_seasonality_sql` shows the identical partial-2026-year pattern (Jan–Jun months each occur 7 times across 2020–2026; Jul–Dec months only 6 times, since no Jul–Dec 2026 data exists yet) — not a real seasonal cycle. Flagged the same way (`month_of_year_seasonal: true` with the caveat spelled out in `score_rationale`).

### 3c. `MEMBER_ELIGIBILITY` — new table, genuine seasonality not a lookback bug (pass 6)

`MEMBER_ELIGIBILITY` (14,271 rows) is a slowly-changing eligibility segment table: one row per member per eligibility "version" (`ELIGIBILITY_ID`, `MEMBER_ID`, `PLAN_ID`/`PRODUCT_ID`/`GROUP_ID`/`NETWORK_ID`/`BENEFIT_STRUCTURE_ID` foreign keys, `COVERAGE_TYPE`/`COVERAGE_TIER`, `EFFECTIVE_DATE`/`TERMINATION_DATE`, `IS_CURRENT_FLAG`, `VERSION_NUMBER`, `COVERAGE_MONTHS_COUNT`, `CHANGE_REASON`, `IS_RETRO_FLAG`/`IS_DUPLICATE_FLAG`). Queried directly rather than assumed: `EFFECTIVE_DATE` has exactly 78 distinct values, all the first of a month, from 2020-01-01 to 2026-06-01 — genuinely monthly grain, the same horizon as `FACT_PREMIUM`/`FACT_MEMBER_MONTH`.

**Not the same bug as pass 4/5, on inspection**: a fixed 90-day lookback would still yield only ~3 monthly buckets here too, so the same window fix applies (`DATEADD(month, -30, MAX(EFFECTIVE_DATE))`, yielding 31 monthly buckets, 2023-12 through 2026-06). But the reason this table needed a closer look wasn't a bug at all — it has **extreme, genuine month-of-year seasonality**: January alone accounts for 12,495 of 14,271 rows (87.5%), consistently every one of the 7 years in the history (not a partial-2026 artifact like `FACT_PREMIUM`/`FACT_MEMBER_MONTH`'s Jan–Jun/Jul–Dec split — this recurs identically across 2020–2026). That's the annual-renewal cycle showing up directly in the data: `CHANGE_REASON = ANNUAL_RENEWAL` is 58% of all rows, and renewals cluster on the plan year boundary.

One KPI added:
- **`main.member_eligibility.count_star`** ("New & Renewed Eligibility Records") — count of eligibility segments/records taking effect each month (new enrollments, annual renewals, plan/group changes). Native/month-grain trend has essentially no linear signal (r²=0.0004) because the January spike swamps every other month, but year-grain shows a real, strong upward trend (r²=0.90, slope +127.5 segments/year) tracking book-of-business growth — consistent with the growth seen in `FACT_PREMIUM`/`FACT_MEMBER_MONTH`. `month_of_year_seasonal: true`, confirmed genuine this time (not flagged as an artifact).

Has a `COVERAGE_TYPE` dimension breakdown (same 4 values as the premium/member-month KPIs). **This breakdown needs a seasonality caveat, not a business-shift reading**: the 4-vs-4-month comparison window happened to place the January 2026 renewal spike inside the *baseline* window (Nov 2025–Feb 2026) and no January inside the *recent* window (Mar–Jun 2026), producing large negative deltas for every `COVERAGE_TYPE` (COMMERCIAL −853, MEDICARE −491, MEDICAID −391, EXCHANGE −268). None of this reflects an actual decline — it's the renewal cycle falling in/out of the comparison window. `snowflake-kpi-anomaly-detector` should lean on `month_of_year_seasonality_sql` and `yearly_trend_sql` for this KPI rather than the native `pct_change_recent`/dimension-breakdown deltas alone; both are flagged explicitly in the manifest's `score_rationale` and `result_preview`.

**A second candidate measure was considered and rejected**: `AVG(COVERAGE_MONTHS_COUNT)` (the average length in months of each eligibility segment) was evaluated but its monthly averages are noisy and only weakly trended (month-grain r²=0.037), driven mostly by small sample sizes in non-January months rather than a real signal. Recorded in `skipped`, not silently dropped.

### 3d. `FACT_CLAIM_DIAGNOSIS` — new table, no lookback fix needed (pass 7)

`FACT_CLAIM_DIAGNOSIS` (221,428 rows) is a **claim-line-adjacent fact table**: one row per diagnosis code recorded on a claim (`CLAIM_DIAGNOSIS_ID`, `CLAIM_ID`, `MEMBER_ID`, `DIAGNOSIS_SEQUENCE` 1–9, `DIAGNOSIS_KEY`/`DIAGNOSIS_CODE`, `DIAGNOSIS_TYPE` PRINCIPAL/SECONDARY, `IS_POA_FLAG`, `SERVICE_START_DATE`, `CALENDAR_YEAR`). Queried directly rather than assumed: `SERVICE_START_DATE` has 2,373 distinct dates, 340 distinct weeks, and 78 distinct months from 2020-01-01 to 2026-06-30 — genuinely weekly/native grain (this table doesn't need to borrow `SERVICE_START_DATE` from a header join; it carries its own).

**Unlike passes 4–6, no lookback-window fix was needed**: a standard `DATEADD(day, -90, MAX(SERVICE_START_DATE))` already yields **14 clean weekly buckets** (2026-03-30 through 2026-06-29), the same bucket count as the other weekly-grain claim tables — confirmed by running the query, not assumed from the table's apparent size or "batch pattern" seen in prior passes.

One KPI added:
- **`main.fact_claim_diagnosis.count_star`** ("Diagnoses Recorded") — count of diagnosis codes recorded per week. Native-grain trend over the 90-day window is flat/noisy (values mostly 670–940/week, with a partial final week at 225), but month-grain confirms real, strong growth (r²=0.83, slope +28.2 rows/month) — tracking the same claim-volume growth already seen in `medical_claim_header.count_star` and `fact_encounter.count_star`, as expected for a claim-adjacent table. Year-grain fit is weaker (r²=0.15, only 7 buckets including a partial 2026). Mild month-of-year variation was checked (Jan–Mar ~19–22K rows/month vs. Jul–Aug ~15K) but is far short of `member_eligibility`'s dominant single-month spike, so it is **not** flagged as `month_of_year_seasonal` or requiring special downstream handling.

Two dimension breakdowns:
- **`DIAGNOSIS_TYPE`** (own table, PRINCIPAL/SECONDARY): both categories grew roughly proportionally in the trailing-4-vs-prior-4-week comparison (SECONDARY 1,709→1,990, +16.4%; PRINCIPAL 1,434→1,578, +10.0%) — no single diagnosis-type driving the increase.
- **`DIAGNOSIS_CATEGORY`** (one-hop join to `REF_DIAGNOSIS` via `DIAGNOSIS_KEY`, resolving to human-readable categories like `ENDOCRINE`/`RESPIRATORY`/`BEHAVIORAL` instead of raw diagnosis codes): growth is broad-based across most categories, led by `ENDOCRINE` (718→795, +77) and `RESPIRATORY` (260→331, +71); `NEOPLASM` was the only notable decliner (47→36, −11), on a small base.

**Two second-measure candidates were considered and rejected**: (1) diagnoses-per-claim (`COUNT(*)/COUNT(DISTINCT CLAIM_ID)`) is nearly constant at 2.13–2.30 across all 14 weekly buckets (CV≈2%); (2) distinct diagnosis codes used per week ranges only 54–70 (CV≈6%). Both too flat to be informative standalone KPIs — recorded in `skipped`. `IS_POA_FLAG`-based rates were also rejected: 83% of rows are null (POA only applies to inpatient encounters), so a database-wide rate would be misleading.

One KPI added:
- **`main.dim_member.count_star`** ("New Member Records") — count of new member profile records created per week. Month-grain regression shows real signal (r²=0.349, growth from 2018-11 into 2019-12), and `month_of_year_seasonality_sql` reveals a striking, real December spike — 473 records in December vs. ~200–260 in every other calendar month, roughly double — plausibly an annual open-enrollment/new-plan-year cohort effect (member records concentrating right before a new coverage year starts). Has a `PRIMARY_SEGMENT` dimension breakdown (4 values, already human-readable, own table — no join needed): `COMMERCIAL` (109→122, +13) and `EXCHANGE` (28→40, +12) drove the recent-window growth; `MEDICAID` (51→46, −5) and `MEDICARE` (33→29, −4) dipped slightly. `COMMERCIAL`/`EXCHANGE` are the same two segments growing fastest elsewhere in this schema (`FACT_PREMIUM`, `FACT_MEMBER_MONTH`), a useful cross-table consistency signal.

**The note's flagged candidate, `AVG(RISK_SCORE)`, was evaluated directly and rejected**: month-grain trend is weak (r²=0.047), and the weekly averages (ranging 1.07–1.69 across the 14-bucket window) are computed over only ~44–66 new members per week — small enough that the swings look like sampling noise from a thin weekly cohort rather than a real population-risk trend. Recorded in `skipped` with this concrete, confirmed reason, replacing the earlier vague "deprioritized for cost" note.

**`DIM_PROVIDER_LOCATION`** (5,148 rows, one row per provider practice location) — `PROVIDER_LOCATION_ID`, `PROVIDER_ID` (FK to `DIM_PROVIDER`), `LOCATION_SEQ`, address columns (`ADDRESS_LINE_1`/`CITY`/`STATE_CODE`/`ZIP_CODE`/`COUNTY_NAME`/`PHONE_NUMBER`), `IS_PRIMARY_LOCATION_FLAG`, `EFFECTIVE_DATE`/`TERMINATION_DATE`. Never examined in any prior pass — profiled fresh. `EFFECTIVE_DATE` spans 2011-10-16 to 2019-06-15 (401 distinct weeks, 93 distinct months, `span_days = 2799`) — the **exact same date range and weekly-distinct-count as `DIM_PROVIDER.ENROLLMENT_DATE`**, suggesting the two tables' dates were generated together. One KPI added:

No real linear trend at month grain (r²=0.0005) or year grain (r²=0.018, 9 buckets) — consistent with the same flat pattern already seen on `dim_provider.count_star` and `bridge_provider_network.count_star`; all three are historical provider-network build-out tables with no organic recent activity. **A second candidate measure was considered and rejected**: `AVG(IS_PRIMARY_LOCATION_FLAG)` (share of primary-designated locations per week) — weekly values range 0.42–1.00 on tiny per-week samples (9–18 locations/week), consistent with sampling noise around a static per-location attribute rather than a real time-varying business rate. **Standout finding 1 — `fact_claim_payment.count_star` is trending down hard.** Weekly payment count fell from ~260–310/week in June–July to 88/week by the week of 2026-08-24 (r²=0.70, slope ≈ −19.3 payments/week, ≈ −34% over the recent-vs-prior-4-week comparison). The latest full week's robust z is essentially at the critical fence (median 266, MAD 34 → crit_lower ≈ 89.6; observed value 88). `sum_paid_amount` on the same table shows the same decline (r²=0.39). This is this database's clearest and most actionable anomaly.

**Standout finding 2 — week of 2026-05-04 is a statistically extreme cost outlier**, not a volume outlier. `sum_total_billed/allowed/plan_paid_amount` on `MEDICAL_CLAIM_HEADER` all spike that week with robust z between 4.1 and 13.0 (plan-paid is the most extreme), and the pattern repeats identically at the line level (`medical_claim_line.sum_plan_paid_amount`, z≈11.0) and in average length of stay (z≈5.0). But `count_star` (claims) and `count_star` (lines) for that same week are unremarkable (z≈2.0–2.3, inside the warn band), and the denial rate that week (9.7%) was actually below the 12.1% average. Read together, this looks like a handful of genuinely high-cost claims (e.g. longer inpatient stays) landing in one week, not a duplicate-load or data-quality artifact — see Attribution below.

**Standout finding 3 — `fact_claim_payment`'s recent decline reverses a 6-year growth trend.** The multi-grain check reruns each KPI's measure at monthly and yearly grain over the *full* table history (2020–2026, span 2,372–2,415 days — comfortably past the 730-day bar for a yearly check) rather than just the 90-day window. For `fact_claim_payment.count_star`, native-grain shows the sharp 13-week decline already reported (r²=0.70, falling); but month-grain (r²=0.45) and year-grain (r²=0.37) both show real, positive growth across the full history. `sum_paid_amount` shows the same pattern, even more strongly (month r²=0.54, year r²=0.60, both positive). **This makes the decline more concerning, not less** — it's not noise reverting to a long-run mean, it's a recent break from an established multi-year growth trajectory. This is the clearest example in this database of why native-grain-only monitoring misses things: at 90 days this metric reads as "trending down"; zoomed out, it reads as "off-trend."

**Standout finding 4 — denial rate is creeping up year-over-year, invisibly at shorter grains.** `medical_claim_header.avg_is_denied` is flat at native grain (r²≈0) and flat at month grain (r²=0.10), which would normally read as a boring, stable KPI. But the year-grain trend is strong (r²=0.83, positive slope) — denial rate has been genuinely rising across the 2020–2026 history. A month-of-year seasonality check (spot-checked for this KPI specifically) found no cyclical explanation — denial rate ranges narrowly from 11.0% to 12.5% across every calendar month, with no visible pattern — so this reads as a real secular creep, not a seasonal artifact. Tagged `kpi_class = error_rate` in the manifest given the measure semantics; `snowflake-kpi-anomaly-detector` should treat this as a standing watch item regardless of whether native-grain thresholds ever trip.

Every other KPI's monthly trend (r² 0.62–0.97 for claim/line/encounter/premium/member-month/diagnosis volume and dollar amounts) is consistent with ordinary book-of-business growth and isn't concerning on its own — it's listed in the manifest for completeness, not as an anomaly. `member_eligibility.count_star`'s year-grain growth (r²=0.90) belongs in this same "expected growth" bucket once its January seasonality is accounted for, as does `fact_claim_diagnosis.count_star`'s month-grain growth (r²=0.83). `dim_member.count_star`'s December spike and `dim_provider_location.count_star`'s flat historical pattern (§3e) are both benign, explained findings, not anomalies requiring action.

**Attribution for the payment-count decline (Standout finding 1) — now a real business-language finding, not just a statistical one.** The correlation-based approach (only 4 overlapping weeks against claim volume, too thin to trust) never resolved this. A **business dimension breakdown** does: joining `FACT_CLAIM_PAYMENT` to `MEDICAL_CLAIM_HEADER` via `CLAIM_ID` and splitting `PAID_AMOUNT` by `CLAIM_TYPE`, comparing the trailing 4 weeks against the 4 weeks before that (equal windows):

| Claim type | Baseline (payments, $) | Recent (payments, $) | Delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ORIGINAL** | 1,006 · $1,143,309 | 466 · $598,554 | **−$544,755** |
| REPLACEMENT | 35 · $38,953 | 42 · $126,800 | +$87,846 |
| REVERSAL | 36 · −$40,685 | 40 · −$116,892 | −$76,207 |
| RESUBMISSION | 54 · $39,374 | 66 · $76,822 | +$37,447 |

**The decline is concentrated entirely in ORIGINAL (first-time) claim payments** — down 54% by count over two months, and this alone accounts for ~110% of the net ~$495,668 decline (the other three categories partially offset it, they didn't add to it). Meanwhile REPLACEMENT and RESUBMISSION payments — corrections and resubmissions of *existing* claims — grew, and REVERSAL amounts (money clawed back) grew too. Read together: **fewer new claims are entering the payment pipeline; the system is still actively working through corrections and reversals on the existing backlog.** This points at claims *origination* slowing, not payment *processing* breaking — a materially different, and more actionable, story than "payments are declining for unknown reasons." Evidence strength: **strong** (≥50%-of-total-change bar, cleared by a wide margin). `AUDIT_LOAD_LOG`/`DQ_RULE_RESULT` would be the next place to check *why* new claims slowed, but weren't examined this pass.

**Attribution for the 2026-05-04 cost spike**: same-week claim/line volume was normal (z≈2.0–2.3) while dollar amounts and average LOS both spiked sharply — consistent with a small number of unusually high-cost claims (e.g. extended inpatient stays) rather than a volume surge or data-quality issue. Denial rate that week was below average, weakening any "bad batch got flagged" theory. Hypothesis, not confirmed cause.

**Attribution for `fact_premium`'s and `fact_member_month`'s growth**: broad-based across all four `COVERAGE_TYPE` segments (see §3a, §3b) — not a single-segment artifact, and the two tables' breakdowns tell a consistent story (enrollment and billed premium growing together, proportionally, across the same segments).

**Attribution for `member_eligibility.count_star`'s apparent "decline"**: not a business-driven decline at all — see §3c. The dimension breakdown's large negative deltas across all four `COVERAGE_TYPE` values are fully explained by the January renewal spike falling inside the baseline window and outside the recent window; the year-grain trend (r²=0.90, rising) is the correct read on this KPI's real direction.

**Attribution for `fact_claim_diagnosis.count_star`'s growth**: broad-based across both `DIAGNOSIS_TYPE` values and most `DIAGNOSIS_CATEGORY` groups (see §3d) — consistent with, and plausibly a downstream artifact of, the same claim-volume growth already attributed to ordinary book-of-business growth elsewhere in this database. No single diagnosis category or type is driving it disproportionately.

**Attribution for `dim_member.count_star`'s December spike and `dim_provider_location.count_star`'s flat pattern**: see §3e. The December spike in new member records is plausibly an annual open-enrollment/new-plan-year cohort effect, concentrated across `COMMERCIAL`/`EXCHANGE` segments; the provider-location build-out shows no real trend or concentration in any single state, consistent with a steady historical roll-out rather than a business event.

## 5. Caveats

- **This was a manual run**, not an automated invocation of the registered `snowflake-data-profiler` subagent — the custom agent definition isn't yet loadable as an invokable subagent type in this session (needs a session reload). The phases were executed by hand against the same instructions. All 37 `MAIN`-schema tables have been checked structurally as of the 2026-09-11 pass. Full distribution stats (percentiles, null rate, frozen-bucket share, seasonality) from the agent's Phase 1 spec still weren't computed for every KPI — only mean/median/stddev/MAD/min/max/slope/r² — that's a depth shortcut, not a coverage one.
- **The usage-driven half of discovery was uninformative through the 2026-09-11 pass, and remains so this pass.** Every non-trivial `ACCESS_HISTORY` hit (including the 21 queries against `FACT_PREMIUM`, 14 against `FACT_MEMBER_MONTH`, 8 against `FACT_CLAIM_DIAGNOSIS`, 22 against `DIM_MEMBER`, and 8 against `DIM_PROVIDER_LOCATION` used to validate these passes' SQL) traces back to profiling-session activity, not organic business traffic.
